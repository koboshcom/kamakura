# Reminder persistence and disk warnings

Schedule success requires an acknowledged durable Mongo insertion. Claims are committed before actual transport send; sent state is committed after send. New deliveries use a durable sending state with a 120-second lease. A failed send or sent-state write stays visible and is retried after lease expiry, including after restart, at up to ten due records per tick. Sending records are never terminal-pruned and count toward the 100 outstanding reminders per chat. Mongo outages fail visibly and the existing one-second reminder loop tries storage again. No memory-only acceptance or database fallback.

Delivery is at least once, not exactly once. A crash, ambiguous transport result or write failure after an actual send can cause a duplicate on recovery. A transport that hangs beyond the lease can also overlap another process; keep one core writer. Historic legacy claimed records remain uncertain and are not replayed automatically. They are not converted to sent. Authorization is checked before claim and before send. A send already handed to a transport cannot be recalled. History-write failure after a confirmed send is logged separately and does not fabricate a send failure.

The existing 15-second storage recovery loop checks statfs on DATA_DIR only. LOW_DISK_WARNING_BYTES defaults to 1073741824 (1 GiB). Low-space/read/config warnings are rate limited to once per five minutes and do not block unrelated operations. An unreadable filesystem is unknown, not healthy. A separate Mongo volume is not observable through this check; Mongo ENOSPC errors remain separately logged, and operators must monitor that volume. No automatic cleanup, filesystem filling, live database wipe or mount changes.
